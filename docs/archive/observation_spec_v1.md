# Observation specification — Metagross milestone v1

Status: approved 2026-11-15; implemented in `engine.c`, with fixtures in `observation_test.c`. Schema: `metagross_observation_v1` (transport `MGO1`, version 1); public rule metadata version 1. Sources: `ASTRA_BRIEFING.md`, current `engine.c`, corrected `README.md`, `IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md` (including section 5 and Amendment), `issue_addendum.md`, and `ablation/findings.md`.

## 1. Principles and frozen scope

This milestone is the Level 50 Metagross mirror in Regulation M-C, with simultaneous selection, the current 17 entries in `MOVES[]`, the current implemented item pool (33 candidate items plus the existing none/stone representations), and `MAX_TURN = 100`. At the cap, `battle()` compares actual HP fractions internally. Public rounded percentages do not replace that adjudication. Mega builds are already Mega at the initial observation; there is no Mega action or delayed evolution. Normal builds remain normal. Existing engine mechanics, including their approximations, are the rule model for this specification.

**Core invariant: identical own state plus identical actor-visible public history implies identical observation and identical legal-action mask, regardless of hidden opponent state.** This includes identical encodings of unknowns and beliefs. With the same independent policy-sampling state, it also implies identical action distributions and sampled actions. A hidden pending choice, hidden build, or different battle RNG state must not change any of these outputs. This invariant concerns the decision boundary; future battle events can legitimately diverge.

An actor receives its own exact battle state, public rules, public events, and deterministic summaries or beliefs computed solely from those inputs. Opponent engine truth is never copied directly into an observation. The event adapter necessarily sees internal resolution, but may disclose only the allowlisted announcements and visible values below. An inferred singleton is still a belief with public provenance, not permission to read the corresponding `Mon` field. No belief may be seeded from the actual opponent or a finalist/build identifier.

Own exact state is an explicit milestone allowance, including own counters. It does not authorize exposing exact damage dealt to the foe, the foe's counters, or battle randomness. Public knowledge is actor-relative: each side sees its own numeric HP and the other side's rounded bar. The observation and value-network input use the same boundary. Eventual outcomes may be training targets; future events, hidden diagnostics, and opponent identifiers may not be actor/value features.

Keep `POL_MIRROR`, `POL_NOISE`, `POL_FAST`, `POL_STALL`, and especially `POL_GENERIC` unchanged and available. Generic is a frozen **privileged baseline**, not an information-matched pilot. Future observable heuristic v2 and `POL_LEARNED` must use the same dedicated observation interface at the selection point presently served by `choose_move(Mon *me, Mon *foe)`. They must not receive either unrestricted `Mon *`, a battle context pointer, `Rng *`, or access to `est_damage(me, foe, ...)`. Legacy policies may retain that signature on their separate path.

## 2. Field-level visibility contract

Classes are `exact` (permitted own information), `public-event` (visible or announced), `belief` (derived only from allowed evidence), and `forbidden`. In the tables, “omitted” means no feature/event field exists, not a zero substituted for a hidden value. All public trackers initialize from a battle-start event, never an opponent memory copy. No mid-battle attachment is supported in v1.

**HP precision provenance.** `issue_addendum.md` records the human's confirmation of rounded integer percentages. Adopt that decision, rather than re-opening precision. The mapping above uses half-up rounding on nonnegative HP (not ties-to-even). Exact half rounding and endpoint rendering are UI assumptions to verify against the real display; verification does not authorize a finer channel. If verification changes the mapping, version the schema. HP loss seen by an attacker is the difference between displayed bar endpoints, not `round(100 * damage / maxhp)`; these are not interchangeable.

## 3. Announcement vocabulary and event provenance

The following is the approved explicit announcement contract (2026-11-15). Current `battle_trace()` is a privileged debug trace, not an observer source: it prints both exact HPs, omits effects, and can attribute Trick failure after resolution using `trick_stone_blocked()` without identifying the actual earlier failure branch. The new event stream must originate at the branch that resolves an effect. It must not infer announcements from a final raw-state diff.

Named item revelations are allowed for:

- Rocky Helmet recoil; Life Orb recoil; Shell Bell healing; Leftovers healing.
- Sitrus/Oran HP restoration, Chesto/Lum sleep cure, Leppa PP restoration, and White Herb stage restoration, each with item identity and consumption event.
- Shuca/Chople/Kasib/Payapa activation when the current `calc_damage()` resist-berry branch actually consumes it. Do not invent activation for an ineffective matchup; the current mirror may never activate some types.
- Focus Sash consumption/survival; Focus Band activation/survival (not consumed).
- Air Balloon's initial floating announcement when intact, its Ground immunity and its pop. A spent balloon is not announced as floating.
- Quick Claw activation when the current equal-priority order branch produces activation, including when both activate; no extra roll for announcements.
- Successful Trick: identities of exchanged items, including absence; each recipient also knows its own resulting item and used state. Transfer the previously known consumption history. Failed Trick is a generic failure unless a public Protect/Substitute event supplies the cause; it does not reveal a hidden stone or item.

No automatic item-name announcement is generated for Scarf's Speed/lock, Muscle Band, Wise Glasses, Expert Belt, Spell Tag, Soft Sand, Twisted Spoon, Black Belt, Metronome, Scope Lens, King's Rock, Wide Lens, Zoom Lens, Bright Powder, or Iron Ball. Those items can still be revealed by Trick. A flinch is public without naming King's Rock; damage/misses/order can support future beliefs without direct item disclosure. Suppressed healing and a no-op at full HP do not reveal a healing item merely because its code was visited.

Clear Body announces on an eligible SF, BD or MS stat-drop block, after the checks that reach that effect. For SB, the engine skips the secondary-effect RNG test entirely when `ability == AB_CB`; do not add a draw or claim that a probabilistic drop was attempted. Conservatively emit no SB-specific Clear Body announcement in v1; absence of a drop is not revelation. Tough Claws is known from Mega forme, not a damage-triggered banner. Light Metal/none produce no banner. These are adapter conventions for the frozen engine, not assertions that all real-game animations have been verified.

Events must carry only: sequence number within battle, turn, actor/target relative seat, event kind, allowed move/item/ability identifier, visible outcome, and permitted value payload. Necessary kinds:

1. `battle_start`: public rules/forme/types, initial HP views, awake/unblocked/no-substitute/zero-stage state, and allowed initial announcements. Each actor's own initialization is supplied separately.
2. `action_opportunity`: visible position in order and outcome (acted, slept, flinched); do not disclose a skipped selected move or priority. If fainting prevents the other opportunity, record incomplete order.
3. `move_used`: announced move ID, direct or Sleep Talk origin, attempted result. Direct misses, Protect-blocked moves and failed status moves still spend PP and reveal that move. Sleep/flinch without a named use does not reveal the submitted choice.
4. `sleep_talk_call`: ST is announced and spends ST PP; called move is separately announced, including failed REST; called slot and called PP are never disclosed and called PP is not spent.
5. `hp_change`: per-side views of before/after HP for **each** direct hit, heal, recoil and Substitute cost, preserving engine resolution order. Foe gets only percent endpoints, self gets own exact endpoints. Record public cause without naming an otherwise hidden item. No hidden damage payload even for Substitute hits; their visible outcome is hit/survived/broke.
6. `hit_outcome`: miss, immunity, Protect/Substitute interception, critical hit and effectiveness where visibly announced, or generic failure. Emit actual crit result from existing `calc_damage()` resolution without additional sampling; no roll value or damage estimate.
7. `stage_change`, `status_start`, `status_end`, `substitute_create/break`, `protect_result`, `item_announce/consume/transfer`, `ability_announce`, `faint`: only authorized payloads above.
8. `turn_end`: completion marker, visible state after `end_of_turn()` healing/flinch clearing and protection reset; tracked opponent state must agree with event replay. This is not a dump of opponent `Mon`. Terminal event gives result and reason (faint/cap), never exact opponent HP fraction for the cap comparison.

All counter updates use the current ordering: flinch is checked before heal-block and sleep decrements; PN may set heal block before a target acts that turn; a sleep counter decrements and still executes the sleep branch even when it reaches zero. Status expiry and action-opportunity events must make this replay unambiguous. Announcements are observational instrumentation only: no mechanics fixes, new random draws or changed effect order.

## 4. Timing, attachment and action mask

Future integration attaches in the simultaneous branch of `battle()`, immediately before the current `choice[2]` / `choose_move()` loop. Initialize two independent knowledge trackers at battle start. At each boundary, after the previous turn's end processing, capture both own-state snapshots and freeze both observations and masks **before either policy is called**. They share a time boundary, not necessarily equal visible values. Then collect both actions, resolve order and effects, and deliver events for the next boundary. Serial callbacks are acceptable; the second callback cannot access the first action or a mutable tracker changed by it. Neither callback sees current-turn Quick Claw rolls or `CUR_SECOND`.

The current policy enum has no observable variant or `POL_LEARNED`; these will be added only in future implementation with `parse_policy()` routing. Keep legacy `choose_move()` and its post-selection behavior intact on the baseline path. The new policy interface uses four own move-slot actions and Struggle (action index 4, mapped to legacy `ci = -1`). No Mega logit. `ENGINE_SEQ` is unsupported for observable policies and must be rejected explicitly, not silently used.

Proposed mask follows selectable own actions, not predicted success:

- A slot is usable iff it is occupied and `pp[slot] > 0`.
- If self holds `ITEM_SCARF`, `choice_lock >= 0`, and `find_move(self, choice_lock)` finds a positive-PP slot, restrict the mask to that slot. This matches the engine override, including `find_move()`'s first-slot behavior.
- If that locked slot is exhausted/missing, release the restriction and allow other positive-PP slots. This is current-engine behavior, even if a later game-accuracy project chooses differently.
- Struggle is legal iff no slot remains usable under those rules. Do not allow it because a heuristic dislikes its moves. Legacy policies can return `-1` early; leave that baseline behavior frozen rather than applying the new mask retroactively.
- Do not mask REST at full HP or under heal block, ST while awake, SUB when its cost would fail, maxed setup, moves facing Protect/Substitute, or status moves likely to fail. These are selectable actions with possible failure. No legality predicate depends on opponent state.
- Terminal observations have an all-zero mask and no policy invocation. Nonterminal masks must contain at least one legal action. Unknown/padded slots are never legal.

**Sleep compatibility is consequential.** `battle()` collects a nominal choice even while asleep and uses its priority for ordering. At execution, sleep ignores that choice and automatically invokes ST if available with PP; otherwise the action is lost. ST chooses among other slots regardless of those slots' PP, and `note_move_used()` records the called move (including failed REST), not ST. Preserve this frozen behavior for the proposed v1 adapter: keep the ordinary mask while asleep, expose own sleep counter, and train on the nominal selected action's actual consequences. Struggle may be selected with no usable PP, but the sleep branch can still prevent its execution. The called move is always an engine event, never a second policy action. For example, asleep nominal PRO can supply +4 priority to an automatic ST call; omitting this fact would silently mis-specify the action space. This follows the already-decided frozen-engine scope; changing it is a separate mechanics change and is not an implementation gate here.

Existing `note_move_used()` updates repetition and Scarf lock even after a move fails. Struggle resets `last_move` and `metro_streak` but does not itself reset `protect_streak`. Trick swaps `item_used` with the item and clears a lock when the resulting item is not Scarf; it does not generally reset repetition. Preserve these details in replay. A policy invalid-action fallback must be logged in diagnostics; recommend deterministic first legal slot (then Struggle), with zero invalid actions required in acceptance tests. Never silently replace a legal sampled action via a different new-policy lock rule.

## 5. Fixed-width v1 representation

No recurrence or unbounded history is passed to either pilot. The observer maintains the following bounded summaries. This is the complete proposed feature layout at field-group level; integer widths/normalization are a serialization detail to freeze with the implementation. Use explicit categorical tags and validity bits rather than NaNs, pointers, raw structs or overloaded zero. Unused payloads are zero; `valid=0` distinguishes unknown from zero. All enum encodings and flatten order must be versioned. Absent, unknown, known none and actual move/item ID are distinct encodings. No model-input schema hash, seed or build identity.

| Fixed field group (in flatten order) | Width / encoding and unknown handling |
| --- | --- |

| Public identity, each side | Species ID, forme/Mega Boolean, two types, six public base stats. Known from start; metadata missing is an initialization error, not substituted final stats. |
| Self numeric state | HP/max HP; six final stats; five stages + accuracy; sleep and heal-block counters; sub HP; Protect streak and protected-now; item ID and used bit; semantic ability ID; choice-lock ID/none; last-move ID/none; exact Metronome streak. All exact, no unknowns. |
| Self move records ×4 | Occupied bit, move ID, current PP, initial/max PP from `MOVES[]`. Padding has occupancy 0 and zero payload. Preserve own slot order to align four logits. |
| Foe visible current state | HP percent, fainted bit; five stages + accuracy; asleep, heal-block-active, substitute-exists; Protect streak and protected-now. All public-event-derived. |

Public repetition tracking is separate from the previous-turn last-announced move: an ST wrapper is announced, but only its called move enters `note_move_used()`. PP records similarly distinguish the wrapper from its call. Struggle is a separate action/event ID and never occupies a revealed moveset record. v1 assumes at most four distinct moves, as in valid four-move builds; duplicate move slots accepted by the permissive parser are outside the training build profile. Such compatibility fixtures must keep PP conservative across indistinguishable slots or reject the build before observation, never reveal slot identities to disambiguate them.

PP range updates subtract one only for an announced PP-spending use. With a known Leppa restoration to that move, add 10 capped at `MOVES[id].pp`. If the announcement does not identify which move was restored, union compatible hypotheses per move and store their enclosing ranges. With complete history and current fixed initial PP, first called revelation starts at full PP unless prior announced uses/restorations say otherwise. If future rules allow several starting PP amounts, initialize the belief to their min/max and update the possible set before taking its hull. Never narrow based on whether the actual opponent can select a move.

The sleep/heal-block sets and Protect/repetition counters summarize all relevant public transitions from battle start. Item/ability/move revelations persist. Order counts summarize past comparisons; their contexts can differ after stages, item transfers or Scarf revelations, so they cannot be combined into a single exact Speed relation. Full ordered damage histories, correlated build hypotheses, numerical damage-derived stat ranges, Bayesian item probabilities, exact positive substitute HP bounds, recurrent memory, and reinterpreting old order evidence after new revelations are deferred. A future damage estimator must range over compatible public-rule hypotheses, including crits, item uncertainty, Substitute and quantization, never select the true hidden defense.

Own Shell Bell recovery or Struggle recoil may constrain damage through public arithmetic. Such deductions are legitimate future beliefs; v1 still does not expose the engine's exact damage roll, nor back-fill an “exact damage dealt” feature from those internal functions.

## 6. Validation and acceptance plan

These are requirements for the future implementation; this spec-writing run does not execute them. Use paired engine fixtures and independently replayable sanitized histories. A pair must agree on **all** authorized own data and history: changing a hidden field that would have produced a different already-visible event is not a valid equivalence pair. Counterfactual unit fixtures can supply identical sanitized histories; reachable integration fixtures must actually produce them.

| Fixture | Concrete scenario and required assertion |
| --- | --- |
| Turn-1 Speed leak | Self S=142, ordinary equal-priority options. Foe S=142 versus S=178 with same public normal forme, unrevealed non-announcing item and 100% bar. Observations/masks identical; no `eff_speed(foe)` comparison. |
| First lost order | Equal-Speed foe wins a tie versus faster foe acts first; choose non-damaging setup moves to match all visible changes. Both histories say foe-first with equal priorities. Observations match and never label one “tie” or “strictly faster.” |
| Priority and effects | PRO versus EQ must not count as Speed evidence. Equal-priority Quick Claw activation is announced and unqualified; both-claw case likewise. Hidden Scarf/Iron Ball remain unknown before an authorized reveal. Sleep nominal priority unseen makes evidence unqualified. |
| HP quantization | Foe 100/200 versus 101/202 displays 50%; choose different hidden allocations/defenses with identical bar endpoints. Numeric foe HP/damage absent. Test half rounding (e.g. 1/200 → 1%), living rounded 0 (1/300), and damaged rounded 100 (299/300), plus explicit faint event. Arbitrary-HP examples are serializer fixtures, not claims of legal Metagross builds. |
| Damage inference | Two hidden defenses/item configurations yield the same visible hit endpoints and crit announcement; same observation and deferred stat bounds. ## 8. Implementation and validation commands (2026-11-15)

```sh
gcc -O3 -march=native -o engine engine.c -lm
gcc -O2 -Wall -Wextra -o /tmp/observation_test observation_test.c -lm
/tmp/observation_test
env -u ENGINE_SEQ -u ENGINE_OBSERVER ./engine matrix 3000 12345 | md5sum

./engine battle '187,155,150,115,110,122|AGI,SB,REST,ST|obsdemo|none|0|cb' '187,155,150,115,110,122|AGI,SB,REST,ST|obsdemo|none|0|lm' 100 12345
```

`obsdemo` automatically enables observation. `ENGINE_OBSERVER=1` enables it for legacy policies; the old matrix requires explicit `ENGINE_OBS_COMPAT=1`. Environment switches are presence-based, matching `ENGINE_SEQ`: unset them to disable. Observation is supported only in simultaneous `battle()`; sequential observation and observable policies in privileged `trace` are explicitly rejected.

Implementation details fixed for v1:

- The canonical transport is 1,032 bytes: ASCII `MGO1`, little-endian unsigned 32-bit word count 256, then 256 signed two's-complement little-endian 32-bit words. Words 0–180 follow section 5; 181–255 are reserved zero. Exact offsets and enum encodings are documented beside `Observation` and the enums in `engine.c`. No native struct padding is serialized. Mask words are also supplied as a separate five-byte callback argument.
- Rule profiles are 1 (regular) and 2 (compatibility). Public base stats come from the checked-in `species-data/species_dataset.json` normal/Mega entries. Regular stat validation uses the project's existing level-50 cores, 0–32 allocation points per stat, at most 66 total, and the 21 nature effects. Under-budget legal spreads are accepted. Compatibility allows positive arbitrary stats, short distinct movesets, and `AB_NONE`; duplicate slots and variable initial PP are rejected in both profiles.
- Public event packets are relative to their recipient (`0=self`, `1=foe`). HP packets are sanitized before delivery. Stage deltas, copy/restoration operations, and bookkeeping markers replay public transitions; no opponent-state refresh occurs at a boundary. The adapter, like the existing `CUR_SECOND`, supports one active battle per process, not concurrent/reentrant battles.
- Preserved legacy `fast`/`stall` behavior can spend exhausted PP. The signed PP ledger continues subtracting public spends below zero in those battles; this is replay of legacy behavior, not permission for observable pilots to select exhausted slots. No mechanics change was made.
- The test program prints a result per fixture, audits replay at every turn end, and compares full battle state, result, turns and RNG for 3,750 observer-off/on legacy pairs. The valid-action acceptance counter must be zero. Two deliberate invalid-output injections test fallback and diagnostics separately, then restore that counter. Variable-PP interval arithmetic is a unit fixture only; variable initialization remains deferred.
